While growing awareness of women’s health has led many women to pay closer attention to changes in their bodies, it is not always easy to fully understand their causes or significance. When facing discomfort or uncertainty, women tend to seek information through the internet and make judgments by comparing their experiences with those of others. Through exposure to similar experiences, women gain psychological reassurance and gradually construct an understanding of their own physical conditions.
This study aims to clarify the processes of cognition, interpretation, and coping in PMS experiences through questionnaire surveys and in-depth interviews conducted with women in Japan, China, and the United States. The results indicate that women’s menstrual experiences constitute a complex process in which bodily data, emotions, and social experiences interact with one another.
