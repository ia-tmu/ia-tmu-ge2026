1c:T637,This study aims to address the issue of "superficial Japanese expression"—characterized by visually obvious elements such as tatami mats, shoji screens, and traditional patterns—frequently observed in recent capsule hotels. By extracting the spatial characteristics inherent in Japanese architecture, particularly Sukiya architecture, this research seeks to establish a design methodology for applying these concepts to contemporary capsule hotels.
Rather than relying on the mere quotation of decorative motifs, this study attempts to reconstruct the quality of the accommodation experience by focusing on essential Japanese spatial qualities, such as yohaku (empty space), ma (spatial/temporal intervals), zure (intentional shifting or misalignment), and the relationship between the human body and space.
Through a literature review and fieldwork conducted at capsule hotels in Tokyo, the relationships among location characteristics, user demographics, capsule formats, and lounge functions were analyzed. Based on these findings, a design proposal was developed for a site located in the vicinity of Ueno Station. By integrating the spatial concepts of Sukiya architecture into both the interior of the individual capsules and their collective configuration, the design aims to create an environment where users can engage with the essence of Japanese culture throughout their stay, even within a minimal accommodation unit.
