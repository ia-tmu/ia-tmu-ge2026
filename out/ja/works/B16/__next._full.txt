1c:T575,This project reconsiders typography not as flat visual information, but as a spatial visual experience that emerges through the movement of the viewer’s perspective. While conventional typography has been designed primarily to be read or viewed on a flat surface, this work focuses on the moiré phenomenon created by overlapping vertical lines. Through this optical effect, letters appear to fluctuate and continuously change as the viewer moves.
Helvetica was selected as the typeface for this project. By using a neutral and non-decorative structure, the perceptual changes caused by the moiré effect itself are emphasized rather than stylistic expression.

The project consists of two developments. One is a series of three-dimensional letters produced with a 3D printer. Three different scales and five formal approaches were established to compare how letterforms are perceived under the same visual principle.
The other is a spatial installation using acrylic panels arranged to form a pangram containing all letters of the alphabet. As viewers move through the space, letters emerge, dissolve, and reconfigure.
