1d:T637,This study aims to address the issue of "superficial Japanese expression"—characterized by visually obvious elements such as tatami mats, shoji screens, and traditional patterns—frequently observed in recent capsule hotels. By extracting the spatial characteristics inherent in Japanese architecture, particularly Sukiya architecture, this research seeks to establish a design methodology for applying these concepts to contemporary capsule hotels.
Rather than relying on the mere quotation of decorative motifs, this study attempts to reconstruct the quality of the accommodation experience by focusing on essential Japanese spatial qualities, such as yohaku (empty space), ma (spatial/temporal intervals), zure (intentional shifting or misalignment), and the relationship between the human body and space.
