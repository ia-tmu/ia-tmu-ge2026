1c:T735,Birds possess a wide range of appealing qualities, including the patterns of their feathers, the forms of their bodies, and their rich coloration. This study focuses on the beauty and rhythmic forms unique to each species as found in these details, and aims to demonstrate new graphic possibilities of birds by extracting and reconstructing these elements as patterns. As the final outcome, 44 original patterns were compiled into a volume titled “Bird Pattern Design Collection.”
The patterns were created using photographs that I personally took as primary reference materials. The production process followed two main approaches: (1) focusing on distinctive patterns found on specific parts of a bird’s body and reconstructing them into a design, and (2) extracting characteristic shapes of birds and combining those forms to create new patterns. In constructing these designs, methods from traditional Japanese patterns were applied, and the names of the works were also derived from traditional naming conventions in order to create a sense of familiarity for viewers.
When compiling the designs into the book, the traditional Japanese dyeing technique of katazome (stencil dyeing) was incorporated.
